The chain bench

Nine sessions built the mechanism a piece at a time. This one puts the whole register in front of you with the buttons an attacker would want. Edit an entry and watch the check fire. Recompute that block's hash to cover your tracks and watch the failure move rather than vanish. Then reforge the tail and see what it cost, which at difficulty zero is nothing at all. That last result is the uncomfortable one, and it is the reason every other part of the design exists.

LiveWhat the bench is doing4 min▶

Each button runs the real engine. Building assembles six blocks, each one hashing its own index, timestamp, entry, the previous block's hash and its nonce. Editing changes an entry and leaves every stored hash alone. Recomputing fixes just that block's hash. Reforging re-mines from block 2 to the end.

Nothing is stored between presses. The digests in the table are computed when you press, which is why raising the difficulty makes the build visibly slower: the machine is guessing.

Part 1 · the bench

Build it, then break it

15 min hands on

Work the four buttons left to right, and read the check line after every press before moving on. The sequence matters more than any single result.

LiveThe sequence worth doing first5 min▶
  • Build at difficulty 0. Six blocks, no problems. Note that block 0's previous hash is a row of zeros, which is a convention and not a computed value.
  • Edit block 2. One problem: a hash mismatch at block 2. The entry changed and the stored hash did not, so recomputing no longer agrees.
  • Recompute its hash. Still one problem, and this is the moment the course turns on: it is now a link mismatch at block 3. The failure did not go away, it moved.
  • Reforge from block 2. Clean. At difficulty 0 that took no measurable work, and the register now tells a different story with no trace of the edit.
LiveWhy the two failure kinds are worth telling apart3 min▶

A hash mismatch at block N means the record at N was edited in place by somebody who did not recompute anything. A link mismatch at block N means block N-1 was rebuilt and its successors were not. They point at different levels of effort and different amounts of knowledge, which makes the distinction genuinely useful when you are looking at a register that has failed rather than reading about one.

Self-studyRun the same edit at difficulty 34 min▶

Repeat the four steps with the difficulty set to 3 and watch the hash count on the reforge. Then do it again. The two totals will differ, often by a lot, because four blocks means four independent lottery draws rather than four times an average.

That variability is the honest shape of the cost. A single reforge total is one draw, and quoting it as "what it costs" is the mistake session 4 spends its whole page on.

Part 2 · what the bench proves

And what it does not

8 min live

The bench demonstrates one guarantee cleanly and is silent on several others. Being precise about which is which is the difference between understanding the technology and repeating its marketing.

LiveThe difficulty zero result is the important one4 min▶

A register with no mining is fully rewritable in milliseconds by anyone who can edit the file. Every hash still chains, every check still passes, and the history is whatever the last editor wanted. Tamper evidence on its own buys you nothing against someone who can write to the store.

What mining adds is not a different guarantee, it is a price. The structure was always rewritable and the work is what makes rewriting unattractive. Saying "it is on a chain, so it cannot be changed" skips the only part that was doing the work.

Self-studyFour questions for a system you are shown4 min▶
  • What exactly is on the chain? Often it is a hash of a document held elsewhere, which protects the document from silent edits and protects nothing else.
  • Who runs the machines? If the answer is one organisation, the tamper evidence is against outsiders and not against the operator.
  • What is the work factor, and who could exceed it? A price only deters people for whom it is a price.
  • What happens when an entry is wrong? If the answer is that it cannot be, the person answering has not run session 5.
Part 3 · build-along

Work the bench, then write the paragraph

11 min hands on

Six steps on the bench above, ending in one paragraph you could send to somebody deciding whether to fund a register.

Run the four button sequence at difficulty 0

Build, edit, recompute, reforge. Write down the check line after each press, in four short lines. You should have: clean, hash at 2, link at 3, clean.

Say what changed between press two and press three

Nothing about the attacker's goal changed and nothing about the entry changed. Only the failure moved. Say in one sentence what that tells you about where the protection actually lives.

Reforge twice at difficulty 3

Note both hash totals. Then say which of the two you would quote to somebody, and why the honest answer is neither on its own.

Put a claim in the right column

Take the sentence "our records are on a blockchain so they cannot be tampered with" and rewrite it so that every part of it sits in the left column of the diagram above. Notice how much shorter it becomes.

Find the step nobody can verify

Trace the Harbourgate entry that says the seal was intact, back to the moment it became true. Identify the point in that path where the register has nothing to offer, and say who is trusted there.

Write the paragraph

What the register would guarantee, what it would not, what it would cost to defeat, and who would still have to be trusted. Four sentences. If the fourth is empty you have missed somebody.

The habit to keep. Whenever somebody says a record is protected, ask against whom. Tamper evidence protects against a party who can edit the store but cannot redo the work. It does nothing against a party who can do both, and nothing at all against a record that was wrong when it was written.

Check yourself

Three questions

You edit block 2, then recompute block 2's hash. The check still reports exactly one problem. What changed?

A · Nothing meaningful; the register reports one problem either way, so the second step was pointless
B · The kind and location moved: a hash mismatch at block 2 became a link mismatch at block 3, because block 3 still records the old hash as its predecessor
C · The problem count stayed at one because the engine reports only the first failure it finds
Fixing the hash makes block 2 internally consistent and pushes the inconsistency into its successor. The failure moves rather than disappearing, which is why hiding an edit means redoing every later block.

At difficulty 0 you reforge the tail and the register verifies clean with the edited entry in place. What does that establish?

A · That the engine has a bug, since a tampered chain should never verify
B · That tamper evidence failed, so the chain structure does not work
C · That the structure alone is fully rewritable, and the work required to rebuild it is the only thing making a rewrite unattractive
The chain was always rewritable. Mining adds a price rather than a different guarantee, which is why "it is on a chain so it cannot change" skips the part doing the work.

A supplier says their register proves every inspection really happened. Which column does that claim belong in?

A · The right column: the register can show the inspection entry has not been altered since it was written, and has nothing to say about whether the inspector looked
B · The left column, because a verified chain means the entries in it are verified
C · The left column, provided the difficulty is high enough
No amount of work factor makes an entry true. Whether the inspection happened is settled by the inspector, and the register faithfully preserves whatever they wrote.
After the course

Homework

An hour, on something real.

Build the smallest chain that works

A hash, a previous hash, and a verify loop that recomputes both. Forty lines is plenty, and writing it yourself is what makes the two failure kinds obvious rather than memorised.

Break it before you trust it

Edit a record and confirm your verify reports it. A check you have never seen fail is not yet a check, and that applies to every validator you will ever inherit.

Take one real claim apart

Find a product page that says a chain makes data trustworthy, and sort each sentence into the two columns. Write down which of the four questions it does not answer.

Cheat sheet

Hash mismatch at N. The record at N was edited in place.
Link mismatch at N. Block N-1 was rebuilt and its successors were not.
Fixing a hash moves the failure. It does not remove it.
Difficulty 0 is fully rewritable. The work, not the structure, is the deterrent.
Quote a mean with its trial count. One reforge total is one lottery draw.
Ask against whom. Tamper evidence is relative to what the other party can do.
A chain preserves, it does not adjudicate. A false entry is kept faithfully forever.
Four questions. What is on it, who runs it, what it costs to beat, what happens when an entry is wrong.
